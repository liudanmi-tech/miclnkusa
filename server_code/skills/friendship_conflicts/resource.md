## Core Frameworks

### Why Friendship Conflicts Are Underserved
We have a lot of cultural scripts for romantic breakups and work conflicts — very few for friendships. This means:
- There's less social permission to take friendship pain seriously
- People often don't know what the "right" move is (confront? drift away? forgive?)
- One-sided effort is especially common and especially exhausting

Friendship conflict is real conflict. It deserves the same care you'd give a relationship or a work situation.

### The Spectrum of Friendship Problems
Different problems call for different responses:
| Problem | What It Usually Calls For |
|---------|--------------------------|
| Single hurtful incident | A direct, honest conversation |
| Repeated pattern | A pattern-level conversation (not just the latest incident) |
| Slow drift | Intentional investment — or an honest conversation about the drift |
| One-sided effort | Name it or accept it — silently resenting it doesn't help either party |
| Toxic dynamic (constant negativity, competition, boundary violations) | Limits or exit |

### The "Too Sensitive" Problem
Being told you're "too sensitive" after expressing hurt is a form of dismissal. A response that validates your feeling while inviting repair: "My feelings are what they are — I'm not asking you to agree that you did something terrible. I'm asking you to hear that it hurt me and let that matter."

---

## Tactical Principles

1. **Say it before it becomes a wall**: The longer you hold something without naming it, the more it calcifies into resentment — and the harder the conversation gets
2. **Address the pattern, not just the incident**: If it's happened before, say so — "this isn't the first time I've felt like this"
3. **Choose repair over being right**: If the friendship matters, the goal is understanding, not winning the argument
4. **Ending a friendship is a valid choice**: You don't have to confront every friendship that isn't working — sometimes pulling back quietly is right. But know which you're choosing
5. **One-sided effort is data**: If you're always the one initiating, always the one adjusting — that's information about how much they're investing

## Sample Scripts

**Naming a hurtful incident:**
> "I've been thinking about what happened at the party and I want to bring it up because I don't want to just let it sit. When you made that joke about my anxiety in front of everyone, it really embarrassed me. I know that probably wasn't your intention, but that's how it landed."

**If they dismiss your feelings again:**
> "I hear that you didn't mean to hurt me. But I'm not asking you to have meant it — I'm asking you to hear that it did hurt. Those are two different things."

**Addressing a pattern:**
> "This isn't just about last week — there's been a pattern where I feel like I'm not quite respected in how we talk about my struggles. I wanted to say something because I value this friendship and I don't want to quietly pull away."

**When you're deciding whether to end a friendship:**
> "I've asked myself: does being around this person leave me feeling good, or does it leave me feeling worse about myself? The answer has been 'worse' more often than not. I think I need to be honest about what this friendship is actually giving me."
