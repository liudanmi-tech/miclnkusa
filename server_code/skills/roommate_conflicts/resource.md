## Core Frameworks

### The Roommate Agreement Approach
Most roommate conflicts stem from mismatched unspoken expectations. Making expectations explicit upfront prevents most problems:
- **Chores**: Who does what, and how often?
- **Quiet hours**: When is it expected to be quiet?
- **Guests**: Overnight guests — how much notice? Any limits?
- **Shared costs**: How are groceries, supplies, and utilities split?
- **Personal space**: Is borrowing things okay? Knocking before entering?

### Address It Early, Not Late
The longer you wait, the more resentment builds — and the conversation gets harder. A small early conversation ("hey, can we talk about the dishes?") is far easier than a blowup after months of frustration.

### The "I" Statement Formula
Frame concerns around your experience, not their faults:
- ❌ "You always leave your stuff everywhere" → triggers defensiveness
- ✅ "When the common area is cluttered, I find it hard to focus — can we figure out a system?" → opens dialogue

---

## Key Principles

1. **Pick the right moment**: Not when either of you is tired, rushing out, or already annoyed — find a calm, neutral moment
2. **One issue at a time**: If there are multiple problems, start with the most concrete one — don't pile everything on at once
3. **Propose a system, not just a complaint**: "Can we agree on a chores rotation?" lands better than "you never clean"
4. **Write it down**: Even a quick shared note or text thread creates accountability and reduces "that's not what we agreed"
5. **Involve an RA when needed**: RAs exist for exactly this — using them isn't dramatic, it's the right resource

## Sample Scripts

**Starting the conversation:**
> "Hey, can we find 10 minutes to talk about how things are going in the apartment? There's something I want to bring up and I'd rather talk about it directly than let it fester."

**Raising the dishes / chores issue:**
> "I've been finding it a bit stressful when dishes pile up in the sink — I do better when the kitchen is clear. Could we figure out a system? Maybe each of us cleans up same day?"

**Noise at night:**
> "I've been struggling to sleep when there's noise after midnight. I'm not trying to kill your fun — could we figure out something that works for both of us, like headphones after a certain hour?"

**When a previous conversation didn't stick:**
> "I know we talked about this before and it didn't really change. I think I need us to be more specific about what we're each agreeing to — can we try again and actually write it down this time?"
