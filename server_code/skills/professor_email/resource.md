# Resource: Emailing Professors

> Injected at: skill activation, alongside baseline_text in system prompt
> Token budget: ≤400 tokens

## Core Frameworks

### The Perfect Professor Email Structure
1. **Subject line**: Specific and clear — "PSYC 302 — Question about Research Paper Guidelines" beats "Question"
2. **Greeting**: "Dear Professor [Last Name]," — always use their title unless they've told you otherwise
3. **Who you are** (if they might not know you): "I'm a student in your PSYC 302 section, Tuesdays and Thursdays at 2pm"
4. **Your ask — one clear sentence**: State exactly what you need upfront
5. **Context**: 2–3 sentences explaining why or any relevant background
6. **What you've already tried** (for questions): Shows you did your part first
7. **Closing**: "Thank you for your time" + "Best, [Your name]"

### Recommendation Letter Requests
- **Ask at least 3–4 weeks in advance** — preferably 6+
- **Ask in person or via a warm email** before the formal request
- **Attach**: your resume, a draft personal statement if available, deadline, and program details
- **Give them an easy out**: "Please let me know if you feel you're able to write a strong letter"
- **Follow up once** if you don't hear back within a week

### Grade / Exam Disputes
- Lead with curiosity, not challenge: "I'd like to understand the feedback better" not "I think this was graded wrong"
- Request office hours — these conversations go much better in person

---

## Tactical Principles

1. **Keep it short**: Professors get dozens of emails — under 150 words for most requests
2. **One ask per email**: Don't stack multiple requests
3. **Do the prep first**: Show you've read the syllabus, tried the problem, or checked the rubric before asking
4. **Respond promptly**: If they reply, respond within 24 hours — it signals you're serious
5. **Thank them after**: A short "thank you, that was really helpful" after a meeting or answer builds rapport

## Sample Scripts

**First-time email asking a question:**
> Subject: BIOL 201 — Clarification on Lab Report Format
>
> Dear Professor Chen,
>
> I'm in your BIOL 201 lecture (Mon/Wed/Fri, 10am). I've reviewed the syllabus and wanted to clarify one thing about the upcoming lab report — should the methods section include error analysis, or is that only for the discussion?
>
> Thank you for your time.
> Best, Jamie Kim

**Requesting a recommendation letter:**
> Subject: Recommendation Letter Request — [Program Name] Deadline [Date]
>
> Dear Professor Lee,
>
> I hope you're doing well. I'm applying to the [Program Name] and would be honored to have your support. Your PSYC 302 course significantly shaped how I think about research design.
>
> The deadline is [date]. I've attached my resume and a brief program description. Please let me know if you feel you're in a position to write a strong letter — and if you need anything else from me.
>
> Thank you so much.
> Best, [Your Name]

**Following up on no reply (after 5–7 days):**
> Subject: Follow-up: Recommendation Letter — [Your Name]
>
> Dear Professor Lee,
>
> I wanted to follow up on my email from [date] regarding a recommendation letter for [program]. I completely understand if your schedule doesn't allow for it. If you're able to, the deadline is [date].
>
> Thank you again for considering it.
> Best, [Your Name]
