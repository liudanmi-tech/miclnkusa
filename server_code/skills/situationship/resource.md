# Resource: Situationship

> Injected at: skill activation, alongside baseline_text in system prompt

## Core Frameworks

### Why Situationships Are Emotionally Expensive
Ambiguity is cognitively and emotionally costly. Your brain can't fully relax into connection *or* fully detach — it stays in a suspended, vigilant state, monitoring for signals. Over time this creates:
- Anxiety that spikes around any perceived shift in their warmth
- A tendency to interpret their behavior optimistically (because the alternative is loss)
- Growing resentment when your investment isn't matched

The longer it continues without resolution, the harder it becomes to leave — not because the connection is deeper, but because your sunk cost increases.

### The Real Question: What Is This Costing You?
A situationship isn't inherently wrong — some people genuinely want something casual and undefined. The problem is when **one person wants more and accepts less** because they're afraid to ask.

Ask yourself:
- If this never becomes official, would I be okay with that in 3 months?
- Am I adjusting my own needs to avoid rocking the boat?
- Do I feel chosen? Or do I feel like the most convenient option?

### The Honest Conversation Framework
If you decide to clarify things:
1. **Own what you want first** (privately): Know your position before the conversation
2. **State it simply**: "I've realized I want more than what we have right now"
3. **Ask directly**: "Do you want the same thing?"
4. **Accept the answer**: Their real answer tells you everything you need to know — even if it's not a clean yes/no

The goal isn't to convince them to want what you want. It's to find out if you're on the same page.

---

## Tactical Principles

1. **You're allowed to want more**: Wanting a label isn't needy — it's knowing what you need to feel secure
2. **"Not ready" without a timeline is a soft no**: If someone can't tell you when or what they're working toward, that's an answer
3. **Actions reveal more than words**: Someone who consistently shows up, introduces you to people they care about, and makes plans is different from someone who's warm in person but avoids any commitment
4. **You can't logic someone into wanting what you want**: If the conversation doesn't go your way, the choice is accept the terms as-is or leave — not persuade harder
5. **Ending a situationship is a real loss**: Give yourself permission to grieve it even if it "wasn't official"

## Sample Scripts

**Starting the conversation:**
> "I've been sitting with something and I want to be honest with you about it. I've realized that what I want is something more defined — I want to actually be your partner, not just the person you see. I care about you, and that's exactly why I need to know if we're on the same page."

**If they say they're "not ready":**
> "I hear that, and I'm not trying to pressure you. But I also need to be honest — I can't keep doing this while feeling uncertain about where I stand. Is 'not ready' something with a direction, or is it a no to this becoming something real?"

**If you decide to end it:**
> "I care about you a lot, and I've thought about this. But staying in something undefined is starting to cost me more than it's giving me. I need to stop — not because you did anything wrong, but because I need more clarity than this can give me."

**Talking to yourself through the decision:**
> "I deserve to be with someone who's certain about me — not just someone who enjoys my company when it's convenient."
