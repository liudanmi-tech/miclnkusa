# Resource: Group Projects

> Injected at: skill activation, alongside baseline_text in system prompt
> Token budget: ≤400 tokens

---

## Core Frameworks

### Set Up Structure Early (Week 1 non-negotiable)
Most group project problems are preventable with early structure:
- **Roles**: Who owns what? (research, writing, slides, editing, presentation)
- **Deadlines**: Set internal deadlines at least 3 days before the real one
- **Communication channel**: One shared place — group chat, Notion, Google Doc
- **Meeting cadence**: When do you check in? Weekly? Async updates?

Teams that skip this in week 1 spend weeks 2–4 in chaos.

### Handling a Social Loafer (Non-contributor)
1. **Direct message first** (not in the group): "Hey, we haven't heard from you on [task] — is everything okay? We need [X] by [date]."
2. **Make the expectation explicit in writing**: "We're counting on you to deliver [specific thing] by [date]."
3. **Document the pattern**: If it continues, you have a record when you go to the instructor.
4. **Escalate if needed**: Most instructors have a mechanism for peer evaluations — use it honestly.

### Decision-Making When the Group Can't Agree
- **Timeboxed discussion**: "Let's spend 15 minutes on this, then vote"
- **Assign one decision-maker** per domain: whoever owns writing decides the structure
- **Separate taste from quality**: Disagreements about style are different from actual errors — let the owner decide on style

---

## Tactical Principles

1. **Document everything in writing**: Verbal "I'll do it" disappears — put it in the group chat or shared doc
2. **Create shared accountability with deadlines**: "Can everyone have their section drafted by Thursday?" is more effective than "let's try to move forward"
3. **Praise effort publicly**: A simple "great work on the research" in the group chat goes a long way for morale
4. **Don't do their work silently**: Covering for a non-contributor without saying anything guarantees it continues
5. **Loop in the professor before it's too late**: Most professors prefer to hear about problems before the project is due, not after

## Sample Scripts

**Kicking off structure in week 1:**
> "Hey everyone — before we dive in, can we spend 10 minutes agreeing on who's owning what and when we're targeting to have a draft ready? I'll set up a shared doc so we're all working off the same version."

**Messaging a non-contributor privately:**
> "Hey [name], I wanted to check in — we haven't seen your part of [task] come in yet. Is everything okay? We're aiming to have it by [date], so wanted to make sure you have what you need."

**Addressing it in the group:**
> "I want to make sure we're all on the same page about timelines. Can we each confirm what we're delivering and by when? I'll write it down so we have a reference."

**Escalating to the instructor:**
> "I wanted to reach out because our group has been having some difficulty with coordination, and I didn't want to wait until the deadline to mention it. Could I come to your office hours to discuss?"
