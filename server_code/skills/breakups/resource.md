## Core Frameworks

### Why Breakups Hurt the Way They Do
Romantic love activates the brain's reward circuits — the same systems involved in addiction. A breakup is neurologically similar to withdrawal: your brain is still expecting the reward (the person) and keeps searching for it.

This explains:
- Intrusive thoughts — your brain is looking for them
- The urge to check their social media or text them — seeking the hit
- The physical ache — this is real, not metaphorical

Knowing this doesn't make it hurt less, but it reframes "why can't I just get over it" into a physiological reality, not a character flaw.

### The Grief Model for Breakups
You don't need to move through stages in order. Most people cycle between:
- **Denial/numbness**: It doesn't feel real yet
- **Bargaining**: Replaying what you could have done differently
- **Anger**: At them, at yourself, at the situation
- **Sadness**: The actual grief of loss
- **Acceptance**: Not "it's fine" — just "this happened and I'm still here"

All of these are valid. The goal isn't to skip to acceptance — it's to move through what's there.

### The No Contact Question
No contact isn't punishment — it's protection. Your nervous system cannot begin to regulate when you're still getting doses of them (a text, a story, a like). The question to ask: "Is this contact helping me heal, or helping me avoid healing?"

## Tactical Principles

1. **Survival mode is okay for a while**: Getting through the day is enough in the early weeks — don't pressure yourself to "be over it"
2. **The rumination loop is not processing**: Going over the same thoughts for the 40th time isn't healing — call a friend, write it down, do something physical to interrupt the loop
3. **Social media is a wound-keeper**: Even passive exposure to their updates delays healing — muting or unfollowing isn't dramatic, it's self-care
4. **Let people in**: The urge to isolate is strong after a breakup — fight it. Even low-energy presence with people who care about you helps
5. **Don't make permanent decisions in acute grief**: Major life changes, reaching out to them, starting something new to prove a point — give yourself 30 days before any big moves

## Sample Scripts

**Telling a friend how you actually are:**
> "Honestly, I'm not doing great. I keep going over it in my head and I can't turn it off. I don't necessarily need you to fix it — I just think I need to talk to someone who won't judge me for still being in it."

**Getting yourself through a bad day:**
> "Today is hard. That's allowed. I'm going to [one concrete thing — go for a walk, text a friend, do one useful task]. I don't have to feel better today. I just have to get through today."

**If you're tempted to reach out:**
> "What do I actually want from this message? If the answer is 'for them to miss me' or 'to reopen things' — the message won't give me that. If I genuinely need to say something for myself, what would I say? And can I sit with not sending it for 48 more hours?"

**After some time has passed:**
> "I'm not the same as I was before this. Some of that is loss, but some of it is actually growth — I know things about myself now that I didn't before. That's not nothing."
