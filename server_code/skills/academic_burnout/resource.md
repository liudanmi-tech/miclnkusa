## Core Frameworks

### What Burnout Actually Is (Maslach Burnout Inventory)
Burnout is not just tiredness — it has three components:
- **Exhaustion**: Depleted physically and emotionally, nothing left to give
- **Cynicism / Detachment**: Stopped caring about things you used to care about; going through the motions
- **Reduced efficacy**: Feeling like nothing you do matters or is good enough

If you're experiencing all three, this isn't a motivation problem — it's a recovery problem. You can't grind your way through it.

### The Burnout Recovery Cycle
1. **Reduce input**: You can't fill a broken bucket. Temporarily lower the demands — drop a class, ask for extensions, reduce extracurriculars
2. **Restore basics**: Sleep, food, movement. These are not rewards for productivity; they are prerequisites for it
3. **Process**: Talk to someone — a counselor, a friend, a parent — about what you're feeling. Suppressing it makes it worse
4. **Reorient**: Once the acute phase passes, address the root cause — is this the right major? The right pace? The right environment?

### When to Escalate
If burnout is accompanied by persistent hopelessness, thoughts of self-harm, inability to function, or significant weight/sleep disruption — go to campus mental health, urgent care, or call/text a crisis line. This is beyond burnout.

---

## Campus Resources
- **Campus counseling center**: Free short-term therapy for enrolled students
- **Dean of students / student affairs**: Can coordinate professor outreach and academic accommodations during crises
- **Academic advisor**: Can help you reassess your load, drop deadlines, or explore leaves of absence
- **Disability services**: If burnout is tied to a mental health condition, formal accommodations may be available

---

## Tactical Principles

1. **Triage before you fix**: What absolutely must happen in the next 2 weeks? Do only that. Everything else can wait
2. **Ask for help explicitly**: Professors, advisors, and friends generally want to help — but they can't if you don't tell them you're struggling
3. **Sleep is non-negotiable**: One extra hour of sleep does more for academic performance than one extra hour of studying
4. **Name what you need**: Sometimes it's rest. Sometimes it's permission to stop pretending you're okay. Sometimes it's a plan. Know which one you need most right now
5. **Recovery is not linear**: A good day followed by a bad day doesn't mean you're back at square one

## Sample Scripts

**Talking to an academic advisor:**
> "I want to be honest with you — I've been struggling a lot this semester and I'm worried I'm heading toward burnout, if I'm not already there. I want to think through my options with you, including whether dropping a class makes sense."

**Telling a professor you're struggling:**
> "I need to reach out because I'm not doing well right now — I've been dealing with some difficult things and it's affecting my ability to keep up. I want to stay in the class, but I may need to ask for some flexibility."

**Getting yourself through the next two weeks:**
> Identify the 3 things that absolutely matter before the pressure lifts. Write them down. Do nothing else.
