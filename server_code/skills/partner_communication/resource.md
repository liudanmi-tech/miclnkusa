## Core Frameworks

### The Four Horsemen (Gottman Institute)
Research on thousands of couples identified four communication patterns that predict relationship breakdown:
- **Criticism**: Attacking the person's character ("You're so selfish") vs. complaining about a behavior ("I felt hurt when...")
- **Contempt**: Sarcasm, eye-rolling, mockery — the most toxic of the four
- **Defensiveness**: Responding to feedback with counter-accusations instead of hearing it
- **Stonewalling**: Shutting down, going silent, refusing to engage

Awareness is step one. When you notice one of these happening — in yourself or your partner — naming it calmly can interrupt the pattern.

### The Antidotes
- Criticism → **Gentle startup**: Lead with your feeling, not their fault ("I've been feeling lonely lately" not "You never make time for me")
- Contempt → **Build a culture of appreciation**: Regular acknowledgment of what you value in each other
- Defensiveness → **Take responsibility**: Even partial ("You're right that I've been distracted lately")
- Stonewalling → **Physiological self-soothing**: Take a 20-minute break — not to avoid, but to regulate before continuing

### "I" Statements vs. "You" Statements
- ❌ "You always make everything about yourself"
- ✅ "I feel like I'm not being heard when we talk about this"

## Key Books

- **The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work** — John Gottman: Conflict isn't the problem; *how* you fight is
- **Hold Me Tight** — Sue Johnson (Emotionally Focused Therapy): Most fights are really about attachment needs — fear of losing the other person, not about the surface topic

---

## Tactical Principles

1. **Don't have important conversations when either of you is flooded**: Heart rate above ~100bpm means rational thinking is offline — take a break
2. **Repair attempts matter more than being right**: A touch, a joke, "I'm sorry, let me start over" — these small gestures prevent escalation
3. **The 5:1 ratio**: Healthy relationships have at least 5 positive interactions for every negative one — conflict feels different when the baseline is warm
4. **Name what you need, not just what's wrong**: "I need to feel like you're on my team" is more useful than "you were rude to me"
5. **Pick the right time**: "Can we talk tonight after dinner?" is better than ambushing them after a long day

## Sample Scripts

**Gentle startup:**
> "There's something I've been sitting with that I want to share. I haven't been feeling as connected lately, and I miss that. Can we talk about it?"

**When the conversation is escalating:**
> "I can feel this is turning into a fight. Can we pause for a second? I don't want to say something I'll regret. Can we come back to this in 20 minutes?"

**Naming a pattern without blame:**
> "I notice that when we talk about [topic], I tend to push more and you tend to go quiet. I don't think either of us is wrong — I just want us to figure out how to not get stuck there."

**After a difficult argument, reconnecting:**
> "I feel bad about how that went. I don't think either of us was at our best. Can we try again?"
