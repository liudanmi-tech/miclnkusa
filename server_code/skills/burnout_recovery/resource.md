## Core Frameworks

### The Burnout Model (Maslach)
Burnout has three dimensions — all three matter for recovery:
- **Exhaustion**: The physical and emotional depletion that comes from chronic demand without recovery
- **Cynicism / Depersonalization**: Emotional detachment from the work and people around you — a protective mechanism that kicks in when exhaustion is chronic
- **Reduced efficacy**: Feeling like nothing you do is good enough or matters

Recovery from all three looks different:
- Exhaustion recovers with rest and reduced load
- Cynicism recovers with meaning, connection, and small wins
- Efficacy recovers with achievable goals and genuine recognition

### The Recovery Paradox
You can't recover while continuing to run at full capacity. But burnout often creates a trap:
- The exhaustion makes you less productive
- The lower productivity creates guilt and anxiety
- The guilt drives overworking to compensate
- The overworking deepens the exhaustion

Breaking this cycle requires accepting a temporary reduction in output as part of recovery — not as failure.

### The Six Sources of Burnout (Leiter & Maslach)
Burnout doesn't always come from "too much work." It can stem from:
1. **Workload**: More than you can sustainably do
2. **Control**: No autonomy over your work
3. **Reward**: Effort isn't recognized
4. **Community**: Poor relationships at work, isolation
5. **Fairness**: Perceived inequity
6. **Values mismatch**: What you're asked to do conflicts with what you believe

Identifying the actual source shapes what recovery looks like.

## Tactical Principles

1. **Complete the stress cycle first**: Before any planning, your nervous system needs a signal that the threat is over — physical movement, crying, deep breathing, or creative expression all help
2. **Triage ruthlessly**: What absolutely has to happen in the next week? Do only that. Everything else is noise right now
3. **Protect sleep like it's medicine**: Sleep is when stress hormones regulate and the brain consolidates — it's not a luxury during recovery
4. **Find one small thing that creates genuine meaning**: Not productivity, not optimization — something that makes you feel briefly like yourself again
5. **Plan the next change, even if it's far away**: Burnout without an exit strategy becomes despair — even a plan 6 months out changes the psychological experience of the present

## Sample Scripts

**Getting through a day when everything feels impossible:**
> "Today I'm not trying to thrive. I'm trying to survive and do three things: [1], [2], [3]. That's enough for today."

**Telling your manager you're struggling (if that's safe):**
> "I want to be transparent with you — I've been running on empty for a while, and I'm worried about my output and my sustainability. I'm not asking for a solution today, but I want us to be able to talk about workload and what's realistic."

**Responding to yourself when the guilt cycle starts:**
> "Doing less right now is not laziness — it's the only way I actually recover. I can't pour from an empty vessel. Protecting my recovery is the most responsible thing I can do."

**Planning the escape hatch:**
> "I can survive [X months] in this situation. In that time, I'm going to [specific step toward change]. Knowing that helps me not feel trapped."
