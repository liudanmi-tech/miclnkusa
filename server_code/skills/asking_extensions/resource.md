# Resource: Asking for Extensions

> Injected at: skill activation, alongside baseline_text in system prompt
> Token budget: ≤400 tokens

---

## Core Frameworks

### What Makes an Extension Request Work
Professors are generally willing to help when a request is:
1. **Early**: Before the deadline, not hours after — the earlier the better
2. **Honest**: A real reason, not an elaborate story
3. **Specific**: A concrete ask ("5 more days") rather than a vague "can I have more time?"
4. **Brief**: A paragraph, not a novel — professors are busy
5. **Action-oriented**: Show you've already started, and that you have a plan

The worst requests are: after the deadline, with no explanation, or clearly fabricated.

### When You Don't Have a "Good" Reason
Poor time management is the hardest case — but honesty still works better than a fake emergency:
- Acknowledge you've been struggling to keep up
- Show what you've done so far
- Propose a specific new deadline
- Accept the outcome gracefully — sometimes the answer is no

### What the Dean of Students Office Does
Most universities have a dean of students or student affairs office that can:
- Provide official documentation for emergencies
- Communicate with multiple professors at once on your behalf
- Coordinate incomplete grades or medical leaves

If you're dealing with a serious situation, going through this office is often smoother than emailing each professor individually.

---

## Tactical Principles

1. **Ask before the deadline**: Even a few hours early shows respect and good faith
2. **Be specific about what you need**: "An extension until next Friday" is easier to grant than "some more time"
3. **Attach documentation if you have it**: Don't make them ask
4. **Acknowledge the inconvenience**: A short "I know this is outside the normal policy" goes a long way
5. **Accept gracefully**: If they say no, thank them and submit whatever you have — burning a bridge isn't worth it

## Sample Scripts

**Medical emergency / illness:**
> Subject: BIOL 201 — Extension Request for Research Paper
>
> Dear Professor Smith,
>
> I'm writing to ask for an extension on the research paper due this Friday. I was hospitalized unexpectedly on Tuesday and was just discharged today. I've attached documentation from the hospital.
>
> I've already completed the outline and about half the draft. Would it be possible to have until next Friday, [date]? I want to make sure I can give this assignment the attention it deserves.
>
> Thank you for your understanding.
> Best, [Your Name]

**Overloaded / honest request:**
> Subject: [Course] — Request for Short Extension
>
> Dear Professor Lee,
>
> I want to be upfront: I've been struggling to manage my workload this week and haven't been able to complete the paper to the standard I want to submit. I've made significant progress, but I need a few more days to finish it properly.
>
> Would it be possible to submit by [new date]? I understand if the late policy applies, and I'm happy to accept that.
>
> Thank you for considering this.
> Best, [Your Name]

**Following up if no reply after 24–48 hours:**
> Subject: Follow-up: Extension Request — [Course]
>
> Dear Professor Smith,
>
> I wanted to follow up on my previous email about an extension for the research paper. I understand you're busy — I just want to make sure it didn't get lost. The deadline is tomorrow, so I wanted to reach out while there's still time.
>
> Thank you again.
> Best, [Your Name]
