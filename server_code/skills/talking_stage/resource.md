## Core Frameworks

### What the Talking Stage Actually Is
The talking stage is an audition period — both people are figuring out if this is worth pursuing. The anxiety it creates usually comes from two sources:
1. **Uncertainty**: You don't know where you stand
2. **Asymmetry fears**: You're worried you like them more than they like you

The healthiest mindset: stay curious about whether *they* are right for *you*, not just whether *you* are good enough for *them*.

### Signals That Matter vs. Signals That Don't
**Meaningful signals:**
- Do they initiate contact, or do you always go first?
- Do they suggest plans, or only agree when you propose?
- Do they ask questions about your life, or mostly talk about themselves?
- Do they follow through on things they say?

**Misleading signals:**
- Response time (varies wildly based on personality and schedule)
- "Warmth" over text (some people are just warm texters — it doesn't mean romantic interest)
- Liking your posts (social media engagement ≠ romantic intent)

### Moving Forward Without Overthinking
You have more control than it feels like:
- **Suggest a second date**: If it went well, just ask — "I had a good time, want to do something again this weekend?"
- **Ask a slightly more personal question**: Depth builds connection
- **Express interest directly but lightly**: "I've been enjoying getting to know you" is low-risk and gives them something to respond to

---

## Tactical Principles

1. **Don't manufacture mystery**: Playing hard to get or waiting artificial amounts of time to reply usually backfires — genuine interest is more attractive than games
2. **Keep your life full**: The talking stage is healthiest when you're not waiting by the phone — your energy is more attractive when you have things going on
3. **Read patterns, not individual messages**: One slow reply doesn't mean they're losing interest; consistent patterns do
4. **Give it time, but not forever**: If it's been weeks and nothing is moving forward — no in-person plans, no progression — it's okay to either ask directly or redirect your energy
5. **Clarity is kinder than ambiguity**: If you're not that interested, it's better to be honest than to keep someone hanging

## Sample Scripts

**Suggesting a second date:**
> "I really enjoyed our coffee the other day. Want to grab dinner sometime this week?"

**Showing interest without pressure:**
> "I've been genuinely enjoying our conversations. I'd like to keep getting to know you in person more."

**Light check-in if things have gone quiet:**
> "Hey, haven't heard from you in a bit — hope you're doing well. I'm free this weekend if you want to hang out."

**If you want more clarity:**
> "I've been having a good time talking with you — I like you and I'm curious where this is going for you too. No pressure, I just figured it's easier to say than to wonder."
