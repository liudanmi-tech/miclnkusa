## Core Frameworks

### Anger as Signal, Not Enemy
Anger is not pathological — it's information. It signals:
- A perceived threat or injustice
- A boundary that's been crossed
- An unmet need (respect, fairness, autonomy)

The problem is not the anger itself — it's what happens next. The goal isn't to eliminate anger; it's to create space between the feeling and the response.

### The Anger Escalation Curve
Anger follows a predictable curve:
- **Trigger** → emotional activation begins
- **Escalation** → rising intensity, reduced reasoning capacity
- **Peak** → flooded state; rational thinking is largely offline
- **De-escalation** → physiological calming, reasoning returns
- **Recovery** → back to baseline

Intervention is most effective in the early escalation phase — before peak. Once flooded, the most effective move is a break.

### The 90-Second Rule (Jill Bolte Taylor)
The physiological surge of an emotion — the neurochemical response — lasts approximately 90 seconds. What keeps anger going beyond that is the story you're telling yourself about the trigger. Naming this gives you the choice to re-engage with the story or let it dissolve.

### The STOP Technique
When escalation starts:
- **S**top — literally pause what you're doing
- **T**ake a breath — slow the physiology
- **O**bserve — what am I feeling? what's the trigger?
- **P**roceed — with intention rather than reaction

- **The Dance of Anger** — Harriet Lerner: Anger in relationships; how it signals what needs to change; how to use it as information rather than a weapon

## Tactical Principles

1. **The break is not avoidance — it's a tool**: "I need to step away for 20 minutes before we continue" is self-regulation, not escape
2. **Identify the trigger beneath the trigger**: Surface trigger (he interrupted me) → real trigger (I don't feel respected) — the real trigger is where the work is
3. **Notice the early warning signs**: Your body signals escalation before your mind does — tension, heat, shortened breath. These are your cues to intervene early
4. **Own your part in the pattern**: Even when someone else is behaving badly, how you respond is yours. "He made me explode" gives away your power
5. **Repair after an outburst**: Accountability matters. "I lost my temper and that wasn't okay — I'm sorry" rebuilds trust faster than pretending it didn't happen

## Sample Scripts

**In the moment, when escalation starts:**
> "I need a few minutes before we continue this conversation." *(Then leave. You don't need their permission.)*

**After losing your temper — repair:**
> "I want to address what happened. I reacted in a way that wasn't okay, and I'm sorry. Whatever the issue was, I should have handled it differently. Can we start over?"

**Addressing the underlying issue (after you've calmed down):**
> "I want to talk about what happened in that meeting. I handled it badly in the moment, and I also want to name the pattern I've been experiencing — I feel like [X] keeps happening, and it's gotten to me. Can we talk about it?"

**Internal reframe when you feel the surge:**
> "I'm flooded right now. Anything I say in this state will make it worse. This can wait 20 minutes. I'm choosing not to hand them my reaction."
