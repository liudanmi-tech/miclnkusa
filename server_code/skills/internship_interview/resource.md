## Core Frameworks

### STAR Method (Behavioral Questions)
Every "tell me about a time when..." question follows the same structure:
- **Situation**: What was the context? (1–2 sentences)
- **Task**: What were you responsible for? (1 sentence)
- **Action**: What did you specifically do? (3–4 sentences — use "I", not "we")
- **Result**: What happened? Quantify if possible, even roughly.

**Key rule**: You don't need work experience to use STAR. Class projects, club leadership, volunteering, part-time jobs, and personal projects all count.

### Self-Introduction: Present–Past–Future (60 seconds)
- **Present**: "I'm a junior studying Computer Science at [school]..."
- **Past**: "Last semester I built [project] which did [outcome]. I also [relevant experience]..."
- **Future**: "I'm really interested in [company/industry] because [specific reason]. That's why I applied for this role."

### The "No Experience" Problem
If you lack work experience, lead with projects and apply the same STAR structure:
- Class project → frame as a real team challenge with deadlines and deliverables
- Club leadership → frame as managing people, budgets, or goals
- Personal project → frame as ownership, self-directed learning, and shipping something

Interviewers recruiting students know you don't have years of work history. They're assessing *how you think*, not your resume length.

---

## Tactical Principles

1. **Prepare 5 core stories**: Leadership, a challenge you overcame, a failure you learned from, teamwork, and an achievement. These cover ~80% of behavioral questions
2. **Research the company specifically**: Know one recent thing about the company — a product, a news story, a mission statement. Generic enthusiasm doesn't land
3. **Ask smart questions**: "What does success look like for an intern in the first 30 days?" shows maturity
4. **Never say "we" when you mean "I"**: Interviewers need to know what *you* specifically did
5. **Follow up within 24 hours**: A brief thank-you email sets you apart from most candidates

## Sample Scripts

**Self-introduction:**
> "I'm a junior studying Computer Science at [school]. Last semester I led a team of four to build a web app for tracking campus events — we shipped it and got about 200 users in the first month. I've also been a TA for intro programming for a year. I applied for this role because I'm really interested in how [company] approaches [specific area], and I think the kind of problems you're working on at scale are exactly what I want to be learning."

**Answering with no direct work experience:**
> "I haven't had a formal internship yet, but in my software engineering class project, we were given 8 weeks and a client brief to build a scheduling tool. I ended up taking on the backend and also coordinating the team's sprints. We delivered on time, and the client asked if we'd continue working on it."

**Asking a good closing question:**
> "What do interns typically work on in their first few weeks, and how much autonomy do they tend to have?"

**Thank-you email (same day or next morning):**
> Subject: Thank you — [Role] Interview
>
> Dear [Name], thank you for taking the time to speak with me today. I really enjoyed our conversation about [specific thing discussed]. It reinforced my excitement about the possibility of joining the team. Looking forward to hearing about next steps.
> Best, [Your Name]
