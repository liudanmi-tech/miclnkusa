## Core Frameworks

### Proximity + Repeated Exposure + Low-Stakes Interaction
Research on friendship formation (Festinger et al.) shows that friendships form through:
1. **Physical proximity**: Seeing the same people repeatedly (class, dorm floor, club)
2. **Repetition**: Running into them multiple times over weeks — not just once
3. **Low stakes**: Small, casual interactions build comfort before deeper conversations happen

The implication: you don't need to be charming or make a bold move. You need to *be around the same people consistently.*

### The Invitation Ladder
Moving from acquaintance to friend usually takes an intentional step up:
- Level 1: Talk in class / after a meeting
- Level 2: "Want to grab coffee before next week's lecture?"
- Level 3: "A few of us are going to [event] — want to come?"
- Level 4: One-on-one hangout by choice, not circumstance

Most people stall at Level 1 waiting for the other person to go first. Someone has to.

### Introvert-Friendly Strategies
- **One-on-one > groups**: Easier to connect, less social energy drain — start there
- **Shared activity > pure socializing**: Coffee while studying, going to a show together, a club meeting — having a "reason" to be there reduces pressure
- **Online to in-person**: Discord servers for your major/interests, campus Facebook groups, or club group chats are lower friction starting points

---

## Key Principles

1. **Consistency beats boldness**: Showing up to the same club for 4 weeks does more than one perfect conversation
2. **Take the small risk**: Most people are happy when someone invites them to something — rejection is rare, and rarely as bad as it feels in your head
3. **Be interested, not interesting**: Ask follow-up questions; remember what people tell you; people feel close to those who make them feel seen
4. **Quality over quantity**: One real friendship is worth more than 10 surface-level connections — it's okay to focus on depth
5. **Give it time**: Real friendship takes months, not weeks. Don't write off a potential connection after one awkward hangout

## Sample Scripts

**Extending an invitation after class:**
> "Hey — I've been meaning to ask, are you going to the study session for Thursday's exam? If you want, we could grab food after."

**Inviting someone from a club:**
> "This has been really fun — are you coming to the next meeting? Want to walk over together?"

**Moving from acquaintance to one-on-one:**
> "I feel like we always talk in class but never actually hang out outside of it. Want to grab coffee sometime this week?"

**If you're anxious about initiating:**
> Start with a low-stakes question that's easy to answer — "Do you know if the professor posts her slides before class?" — then build from there.
